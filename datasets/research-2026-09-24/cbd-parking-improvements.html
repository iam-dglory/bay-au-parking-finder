    Central City (CBD) parking improvement works

    3. Central City (CBD) parking improvement works
    Explore how we're making parking simpler and more available in the CBD.

    We completed the roll out of improved parking conditions in the CBD in March 2024.

    We’ll give the community time to adjust, then review the changes to make sure the new parking conditions are working well.

    Some areas of West Melbourne were updated with the CBD in 2024. Due to community feedback, these areas will now be part of the West Melbourne parking area and will be updated in 2025 in line with the West Melbourne Parking Review. Read more about works in West Melbourne.

    CBD parking improvements

    Consistent two-hour (2P) time limit for all general parking spaces in the CBD, from 7am to 10pm every day.

    • This changes a range of time limits in the CBD to two hours, simplifying parking conditions and the signs that communicate them.
    • A two-hour time limit keeps more spaces turning over for people who need to park at the kerbside.

    Need to run a quick errand? Pay nothing for a short trip.

    • When you use the EasyPark app you’ll pay nothing for a short stop (up to 15 minutes in any ‘green-signed’ parking space, like ‘2P Meter’). If you’re in and out within 15 minutes, we waive the parking fee.
    • No fees are waived if you park longer than 15 minutes. 
    • This initiative helps to boost parking turnover.

    Off-peak parking rate of $4 per hour in the CBD after 7pm on weekdays and all weekend.

    • The peak rate will remain at $7 per hour and apply to metered spaces until 7pm on weekdays. Demand has increased during these periods and this helps maintain parking turnover and availability for people needing to travel by car.

    Loading zones reviewed and repositioned to make sure we have the right spaces in the right locations.

    • All loading zones will be a consistent 30 minutes to give drivers enough time to complete deliveries.

    Accessible parking spaces reviewed and repositioned to improve location and accessibility.

    • Disability parking permit holders can also park for double the time limit shown on any general ‘green sign’ parking space (up to four hours in a ‘2P Meter’ space).

    Designated pick up / drop off spaces in every street.

    • These are typically the first spaces after every intersection. 
    • You can use these ‘No Parking’ spaces to stop for up to two minutes if you stay within three metres of your vehicle.
    • These spaces are easy to find and access, and perfect for if you’re picking-up or dropping-off passengers. 

    Hundreds of spaces ‘unlocked’ across the CBD to make sure more public parking is available at the right time of day.

    • We’ve also set parking conditions so that most loading zones become regular two-hour parking spaces after 4pm. This creates more parking options, more often.

    FAQ

    Why has parking changed in the CBD?

    In 2023, the City of Melbourne introduced the Parking and Kerbside Management Plan. As part of the plan, we’re reviewing the on-street parking in all neighbourhoods across the City of Melbourne. As our city continues to grow and faces more pressure on on-street parking, we need to make adjustments to make sure parking is working for businesses, residents and visitors now and into the future.

    I didn’t hear about the engagement – can I still have my say?

    You can have your say at any time via our comprehensive neighbourhood parking survey which is designed to make sure that community experience is thoroughly considered when we review parking needs in the area.

    We will return to CBD in 2025 to conduct the next annual parking review, which will be informed by community feedback.

    Why are CBD parking improvements different to other neighbourhoods?

    Each neighbourhood across the municipality has its own unique characteristics and on-street parking space usage. We will adjust a range of parking controls to make sure the limited parking on our streets functions efficiently and reduces parking pressure in your local area. Some streets will have more changes than others – it will depend on the parking demands in each local area.

    What happens after the changes?

    These changes won't be 'set and forget' - we know that it can take several months for parking behaviours to adapt, so we'll give the community time to adjust and we will review the performance of the new conditions each year and fine-tune them where necessary. Through this process, parking conditions may evolve over several years.

    How can I access free 15-minute parking in the Central City?

    The EasyPark app allows anyone to take advantage of the ‘15-minute free parking’ in any green-signed parking space. This way you can stop in any available paid parking space (like ‘2P Meter’) and use the EasyPark app to start a parking session at $0 fee for up to 15 minutes.

    If you need to drop off or pick up people or goods, you can also use ‘No Parking’ spaces. A ‘No Parking’ sign means you are not allowed to park and leave your vehicle in that area, but you can stop for a maximum of two minutes to quickly drop off or pick up passengers or goods - as long as you stay within three meters of your vehicle. This makes spaces more available and helps keep vehicles moving and space available for short stops.

    You can have your say any time on parking in your neighbourhood by completing our always on survey. The next review of your neighbourhood will use this data to inform future changes. We will undertake a comprehensive review of how these changes are functioning next year.

    How your information is used depends on the type of activity you are participating in, as outlined below.

    Community engagement projects (consultations)

    Feedback you provide through community engagement projects, including any demographic details you choose to share, will be given to the relevant City of Melbourne work area responsible for managing the subject matter or project your feedback relates to.

    Content that you post on the site publicly will be viewable by anyone who uses the site and will display your username. Any publicly available reports from consultations may include quotes that you have shared in forums, surveys and other consultation tools.

    The City of Melbourne has created a central repository (the Knowledge Bank) of most survey responses. The City of Melbourne seeks to maintain your anonymity within the Knowledge Bank. Any personally identifiable information that you provide, including name and contact details, will be excluded or deidentified within the Knowledge Bank to preserve your anonymity.

    Where you have consented, staff members will also add any verbal feedback you provide to them, in their interactions with you, to the Knowledge Bank. Staff members can filter the responses in the Knowledge Bank by subject matter, neighbourhood or demographics to inform future projects, gain insights into neighbourhood sentiment or key themes, and to ensure the provision of consistent service delivery across the organisation.

    To further protect your anonymity, where there are very small sample sizes, staff cannot filter in the Knowledge Bank by demographics.

    While the City of Melbourne takes multiple measures to protect the anonymity of survey respondents, we ask that you help us reduce the risk of inappropriate information access and exposure by not including other information in your survey response that could possibly identify you or others.

    Expressions of interest and event registrations

    Participate Melbourne also collects personal information through Expressions of Interest (EOIs), event registrations, advisory groups or similar participation activities.

    For these activities:

    • Your personal information (including your name, contact details and any additional information you provide) will be shared with the relevant City of Melbourne project team responsible for administering the activity.
    • Your information may also be shared with consultants (see ‘Third parties’ below) or delivery partners engaged to support the project or activity.
    • Your personal information will not be added to the Knowledge Bank.
    • Information is collected and used for the purpose of assessing your EOI, administering the event or activity, and communicating with you.

    Moderation

    We monitor the information that you provide in order to protect the integrity of the discussions from individuals and groups who may attempt to unduly influence the outcomes of a consultation process. For example, we check to ensure multiple contributions are not coming from the same source. We also review the site to protect it from spam or misuse.

    Third parties

    We may disclose personal information to third-party research consultants engaged by the City of Melbourne. You consent to us disclosing your personal information to the consultants for community engagement projects (consultations), Expressions of interest and event registrations as outlined below under the respective headings.

    Community engagement projects (consultations)

    For community engagement projects, the information supplied to the research consultants is limited to raw feedback responses and excludes your name, contact details or other information that could directly identify you. However, it may include demographic details such as your age, gender and suburb.

    The insights derived by these research consultants will assist the City of Melbourne to identify opportunities to improve our services, projects, plans and policies.

    Expressions of interest and event registrations

    For expressions of interest and event registrations, your name, contact details and any additional information you provide may be shared with relevant project teams and consultants for the purposes of assessing the submissions or supporting the delivery of events or activities.

    If you register for an event or activity through a third-party platform such as TryBooking, your registration details will be collected by that platform in accordance with its privacy policy. Registration details will be shared with the relevant City of Melbourne work area and may be shared with consultants for the purpose of administering the event.

    Planning scheme amendments

    In the case of submissions you make on planning scheme amendments, City of Melbourne will disclose your entire submission, including your contact details, to Planning Panels Victoria within the Department of Transport and Planning. Submissions and contact details are stored in a Panel Book (typically a OneDrive file shared between all parties to the panel, where it is stored for three months before deleting).

    Planning Panels Victoria will facilitate all contact with you on matters relating to the panel process including to circulate agendas, inform you of the date and time of the panel hearing and to send correspondence to you on any other matters relating to the relevant planning scheme amendment.

    All third parties have agreed to the collection, use, and disclosure of data in accordance with this Privacy Statement. The City of Melbourne will not disclose your information to another external party without your consent, unless required or authorised by law.
