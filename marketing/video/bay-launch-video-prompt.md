# Video prompt: "Bay — Never Guess Where to Park"

A ready-to-paste prompt for a text-to-video model (Sora, Veo, Runway, etc.) or a human video brief. Built around a ~45-60 second arc: intro → problem → discovery → resolution → outro.

**Format:** 45-60 seconds, 16:9 (or shoot a 9:16 vertical cut for social), cinematic realism, natural daylight, handheld-but-steady camera feel, warm-to-cool color grade shifting from frustrated (grey/blue) to relieved (warm/golden) as the story resolves.

**Setting:** Melbourne CBD, Australia — real signifiers throughout: green-and-white Victorian street signs, trams passing in the background, laneway cafes, a Victorian number plate, "2P," "LOADING ZONE," and "Pay Stay" parking signs visible on poles.

---

## SCENE 1 — INTRO (0:00–0:05)
Black screen. A single white parking-sign icon (rounded square with a "P") fades in, center frame, pulses once like a heartbeat. Soft ambient synth swell. Text fades in beneath it: **"Every day, Australians waste hours looking for a parking spot they're legally allowed to use."** Cut to black.

## SCENE 2 — THE PROBLEM (0:05–0:20)
Wide shot: a mid-30s driver ("Sam") in a modest sedan, driving slowly down a Melbourne CBD street, midday. Tram bell dings in the background. Sam leans forward, squinting up through the windscreen at a parking sign stacked with THREE different plates ("2P," "LOADING ZONE 7AM-9:30AM," "PERMIT ZONE 3"). Cut to a close-up of Sam's confused face, then a close-up of the sign itself — cluttered, contradictory, small print.

Sam pulls in anyway, hesitates, checks the time on their phone (11:42am), unsure if the loading zone restriction has ended. Sighs, pulls back out into traffic. Quick montage (3 fast cuts, handheld, slightly shaky): circling the block twice, passing a parking inspector writing a ticket on someone else's windscreen, glancing at a dashboard clock ticking forward. Voiceover (calm, slightly wry Australian accent):

> **VO:** "Between confusing signs, fines, and just not knowing if a spot's actually free right now — parking in Australian cities shouldn't feel like guesswork."

## SCENE 3 — THE DISCOVERY (0:20–0:28)
Sam, now stopped at a red light, frustrated, taps their phone. Close-up of the phone screen: the **Bay** app icon (dark navy rounded square with a white parking-square glyph) is tapped open. Screen recording style insert: the app opens straight to a clean map centered on Sam's location, a soft pulsing blue dot marking "you are here," dashed boundary circle showing search coverage, and clustered pins nearby.

> **VO:** "This is Bay — a free app built to show real parking rules, in plain English, right where you are."

## SCENE 4 — THE APP IN ACTION (0:28–0:42)
Screen-capture-style shots of the app, intercut with Sam glancing between phone and street:
- Sam taps a green pin near their location. A sheet slides up: **"Little Bourke Street — Free right now · until 6:30pm."** clean status badge, no clutter.
- Cut to Sam's face, visibly relieved.
- Quick insert: Sam taps another pin showing an amber "Unconfirmed report" badge, then one showing a red "Paid parking, $3.20/hr" — showing the range of honest, real-time information, not just green lights everywhere.
- Sam drives to the spot Bay pointed to, parks cleanly, steps out, glances at the actual physical sign on the pole to double check — it matches exactly what the app said.

> **VO:** "No more second-guessing the sign. No more circling the block. Bay tells you what's actually allowed, and whether it's actually free — built from real council data, checked by real drivers like you."

## SCENE 5 — THE RESOLUTION (0:42–0:50)
Sam locks the car, exhales, smiles — a small, genuine moment of relief, not exaggerated. Time-lapse-style shot of Melbourne CBD life continuing around them: pedestrians, a tram gliding past, golden late-afternoon light now (color grade has shifted warm). Sam walks off toward their destination, phone pocketed, completely unbothered — the problem is just... gone.

## SCENE 6 — OUTRO (0:50–0:58)
Cut to a clean, static brand card: dark navy background, the Bay logo (white rounded square, dark "P"/parking-square glyph) centered, with the wordmark **"Bay"** beneath it in clean sans-serif, and tagline: **"Find real parking, everywhere."** Small text beneath: **"Free. Australia-first. Get it now."** Soft synth chord resolves. Two-second hold, then fade to black.

---

**Voiceover tone:** Warm, conversational Australian accent, slightly understated — not a hard-sell ad voice, more like a friend explaining something genuinely useful.

**Music:** Starts sparse and slightly tense (Scene 2), builds and lightens through Scene 4, resolves into a warm, optimistic, minimal synth/piano theme by Scene 5-6.

**On-screen text style:** Clean sans-serif (matches app UI — think system font, not decorative), white or dark slate depending on background, appears at the bottom third, never covering faces or the phone screen.

**Do NOT include:** Real brand logos of competitors, real people's faces if this becomes a real shoot with actors who haven't consented, exaggerated or slapstick comedy — the tone should stay grounded and true-to-life since the core message is credibility and trust in the data.

## Notes for whoever generates this

- Use the actual logo file at `../brand-assets/bay-logo.png` for Scene 6's brand card.
- If your video tool needs shorter per-shot prompts (most current text-to-video tools generate 5-20 second clips, not a full continuous narrative), split this into 6 separate generations — one per scene — and edit them together, rather than trying to get one model call to do the whole arc.
- A tighter 15-20 second cutdown for Instagram/TikTok would use Scenes 2 (trimmed), 3, 4 (trimmed), and 6 only.
